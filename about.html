About

Started with a full-stack web development course and a physical notebook covering everything from HTML structure to JavaScript events - both still on the shelf. Web development and client projects came before any formal coursework, which made the transition into a university engineering program feel less like starting from zero.

Enrolled at the University of Kansas as a Computer Science student, then switched to Cybersecurity Engineering. Security demands understanding how systems work at every layer - not just how to use them. That discipline is harder to skip in security than in general software development, and that's the point.

Two years of building fast with AI assistance produced real products: a shipped iOS app, freelance client sites, an LLM-powered assistant at a company. It also made the limits clear. AI can generate code. It cannot own a codebase. The move to security engineering is partly a course correction - learning the fundamentals that make fast building sustainable and the output trustworthy.

Projects have shipped on Render's free tier, shared PHP/MySQL hosting on Bluehost, the iOS App Store, and a self-hosted Raspberry Pi running Suricata, Loki, and Grafana over Tailscale. Different deployment contexts, different constraints, different lessons.
